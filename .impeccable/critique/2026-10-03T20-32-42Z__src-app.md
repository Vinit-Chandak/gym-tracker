## 1. Design specificity verdict

**Authored for Overload. A neighbouring tracker could not use it unchanged.**

- **The prints carry the identity.** The colour on screen is a print drawn from the athlete's own
  records, in a grammar that runs everywhere:
  - Today's day, Training's week of prints, the workout under way, the Summary, a day, the
    calendar's marks, History and Friends.
  - The forms are a block for load, a track for a run, a wheel for a ride, waves for a swim, a fan
    for mobility, and a bowl for food.
  - The chrome stays black and white, and the Progress charts are drawn in ink.
- **The log is the strongest single idea.** It is written in the lifter's own shorthand
  ("W 25 × 8 · 35 × 5 · 45 × 3", then "1 60 × 4 @ 2"). The entry stands under it on the same grid,
  so the next set reads as the log's next line.
- **The rest of the language is product-specific too.** Seven squares for the cycle, a dial for
  rest, Jost figures over Atkinson words, and a bowl that heaps when the day goes over.

**Where it slips back towards generic, or loses its own signature:**

- **States with nothing to draw are left empty.** An ad hoc workout opens on a blank paper (C-8).
  On 3 October the overview's month is a sheet of paper with three marked days.
- **Some secondary pages are stock components.** The Strength section, Profile and the forms use
  standard segmented controls, selects and settings rows. They're consistent but anonymous, and
  the segmented control breaks words (C-11).
- **The art's grammar is never taught.** Only the calendar and the Welcome page carry a legend.
  The signature reads as decoration until it is learned.
- **Missed opportunity.** The Summary's records ("Est. 1RM 122.5 kg · was 116.7 kg") sit under a
  print that does not point at the set that earned them.

## 5. Emotional journey

- **Peak.** The set landing is the moment the design was built for, and it works:
  - Save presses and reads Saving…, then the new line rises into the log (220 ms) and Save reads
    "✓ Saved".
  - On the same beat the entry turns to "Set 4 of 4" with the suggestion back in grey and the rest
    dial restarts full at 3:00 (`row2.png`).
  - Nothing in the entry moves. It's quiet, exact and repeatable every set.
- **End.** The Summary is a strong close. The session's own print is on top, then 70 min ·
  13 sets · 7,164 kg, then "4 records" in ink ("Est. 1RM · was 116.7 kg → 122.5 kg"), then Done
  (`row8.png`). The print is the souvenir.
- **Valleys:**
  - An ad hoc session begins on a blank print (C-8).
  - Opening Today with a session already open offers "Start workout", which goes somewhere else
    (C-9).
  - Every superset round becomes back, find, open (C-5).
  - After the last planned set, nothing says "done". The entry just offers "Set 5", and Complete
    sits in a corner menu (C-3).
  - At 200% text the sets vanish behind the entry, and the landing happens where nobody can see it
    (C-2).
  - Before dinner, Food makes you do arithmetic (C-6).
  - Strength reads "Volum e" (C-11).
- **High-stakes moments:**
  - **A save that fails.** Excellent:
    - The entries stay in ink and nothing is added. Rest is left alone.
    - The plain sentence sits over Save as an alert, and Save becomes Retry ("Retry saving set 3").
    - Leaving keeps the draft. Finish waits with a clear sentence, and a reload brings the draft
      back.
    - The one flaw is that the message pushes the steppers up 52 px (C-4).
  - **Deleting the account.** Honest copy ("Nothing is exported first"). Typing DELETE is required,
    and the outlined Delete everything stays grey until then. At 200%, scrolled to the end, the
    tab bar covers half of it (C-10).
  - **Finishing a workout.** Reassuring. Finish lists what's recorded and what's not done, takes
    optional notes and an optional body weight, and its body-weight value is a placeholder that is
    never submitted (finish-form.tsx:56-61).
  - **Deleting a set.** One tap, no undo. This is inherited and noted as context only.

## 6. What's working

1. **The log and the entry share one grid.**
   - Each figure in the entry stands over the same figure in every logged line (load centres 74/74
     at 320, 84/84 at 360, 86/86 at 375).
   - A five-character load (102.5) steps the whole log down the ramp to 26 px at 320 and 28 at 360
     without touching the set number (walk14).
   - At 320 the operators stack + over and − under, and the columns still hold (`w16-320.png`).
   - The result reads like the athlete's own training diary, with the next set already pencilled
     in.
2. **The save is honest and the failure is graceful.**
   - Nothing is drawn before the server answers. A landed set rises into place, Saved holds for
     half a second, and the entry turns over with rest restarted.
   - A failure keeps every figure, adds nothing, leaves rest alone, and names the cause and the
     way out in the app's own words.
   - Drafts survive leaving the logger and reloading the page (walk18).
   - This is the error-recovery heuristic at 4.
3. **The art system is coherent and disciplined.**
   - Prints are centred to the pixel and stand on one baseline.
   - One pigment per family of sport, used for nothing else. Charts are ink, and dark mode keeps
     the art quieter than the white primary.
   - The same marks carry meaning from Today to the calendar to History to Friends.
   - It is the clearest expression of "the art is your training" and the reason the product feels
     authored.

## 7. Issues, P0 to P3

Two P1, ten P2 and four P3. There are no P0s: no task is blocked.

### P1

**C-1 [P1] Screen readers hear nothing when a set is saved.**

- **Screens:** log, moment-saved, superset-log (`/preview/logging`, `?scenario=superset`) and
  log-seeded (`/workouts/f1967ed2-…?exercise=62a072d6-…`); every configuration.
- **Evidence:**
  - During a save the only live region on the page is the rest timer, which has `aria-live="off"`
    (walk1).
  - Focus stays on the Save button. Its text changes Save → "Saving…" (+60 ms) → "Saved"
    (+700 ms) → "Save" (+1.3 s).
  - Meanwhile the heading turns from "Set 3 of 4" to "Set 4 of 4" and a button "Set 3: 60
    kilograms, 4 reps, 2 reps in reserve. Edit" is added to the list. None of it is announced.
  - Code: `exercise-logger.tsx:715-759` (Saving… and Saved are button text only) and `:655-707`
    (the slot speaks only failures and notes).
  - This is a regression: on `main`, `set-grid.tsx:200-211` announced "Saving set N" and "Set N
    saved" through `role="status"`.
  - WCAG 2.2 SC 4.1.3 Status Messages (AA).
- **Why it matters:** the signature moment exists to confirm that the set was written. A blind or
  low-vision lifter gets no confirmation and will save twice or doubt the record.
- **Fix:** put one visually hidden `role="status"` region inside `.entry`. It says "Saving set 3"
  on press and, on the server's answer, "Set 3 saved: 60 kilograms, 4 reps, 2 reps in reserve.
  Set 4 of 4 next." Keep the failure sentence as `role="alert"`.

**C-2 [P1] At 200% text the log opens at its top and a saved set lands out of sight.**

- **Screens:** log, log-warmups, moment-saved and log-seeded at t200 (`/preview/logging`).
- **Evidence:**
  - `.session-body` stays at scrollTop 0 (scrollHeight 449, clientHeight 294; walk6).
  - The entry begins at y 358 while sets 1 and 2 sit at y 385 and 445, so both are behind the
    entry and the warm-up line is cut in half (`w6-01-t200.png`).
  - After Save, set 3 lands at y 505, also hidden, and scrollTop is still 0
    (`w6-02-t200-after-save.png`). Scrolled to its end, the log reads well
    (`w6-03-t200-scrolled-end.png`, `row9.png`).
  - DESIGN.md:513-515 says the title, tabs and log scroll "kept at their end". Instead
    `session.css:637-642` scrolls "from the top, so the first screen names the exercise", as the
    Log-200 board draws it.
  - At default text the log does follow each landing at 320 and 375 (walk16), so only the folded
    layout fails.
- **Why it matters:** the low-vision athlete loses both the record of what they've lifted and the
  landing that confirms it. With C-1, a screen-reader user at 200% gets no confirmation at all.
  Review J flagged the 200% log as a P1 on the canvas.
- **Fix:** keep opening from the top, so the name is read once. After every landing, and on
  returning to the Log tab, scroll the landed line into view above the entry (scroll
  `.session-body` so the line's bottom meets the entry). Show the exercise's name in the session
  header while the title is scrolled away. Correct the Log-200 board to match DESIGN.md.

  (`exercise-logger.tsx:841-863`).

### Questions to consider

- What if the slot over Save were always there, reserved and silent, so that the entry truly never
  moves, for any reason?
- What if a superset's slot were the hand-off itself ("Then Wrist curl ›"), so a round costs two
  taps instead of six?
- Should the landing of the last planned set be the exercise's own small summit: a check, "All 4
  sets in", and the next exercise one tap away?
- Should the entry carry forward what the athlete just did, rather than reset to the rule's
  suggestion, when they deviated from it?
- Could the kcal figure borrow the macros' notation ("1,512.6 / 2,300") and settle the conflict
  between the note and the inventory without a second hero figure?
- Should the overview show the last four or five weeks rather than the calendar month, so that
  the first days of a month are not three marks on a blank sheet?
- Would one quiet legend under the first print an athlete ever sees (as the calendar has) turn the
  art from decoration into language?
